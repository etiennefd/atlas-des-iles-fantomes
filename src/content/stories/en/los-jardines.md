***

Half an hour before the greatest fuckup of my entire life, I was standing outside the bathroom of Residential Unit 1, aboard a giant man-made island tethered to the Hawaiian coast, while the richest man in the world was inside with his girlfriend. They were testing out the water pressure of the faucet.

"Nice water pressure," I heard the richest man in the world say.

It *was* nice water pressure, thanks to the excellent work of the hydraulics engineering team. If the richest man in the world asked, I was ready to give him a precise technical explanation of the entire water system, from desalination to recycling, in whatever level of detail and brevity he desired. I had trained hard for this, for three extremely intense days. I knew everything about every subsystem of the Los Jardines Floating Island Complex. I was ready for anything.

He didn't ask. He and his girlfriend just got out of the bathroom and followed me to the kitchen, where they proceeded to open some of the cupboards. They were empty.

I looked at the girlfriend. She was a top model, obviously. From a Middle Eastern country, I forget which. She hadn't said anything yet, except "mmh-mmh." Whenever the richest man in the world commented on anything, she would make that sensual pouting face that one readily associates with top models, and go, "mmh-mmh."

Not that his comments were any more interesting. The closest he'd come to saying anything that wasn't utterly boring was when he got off the helicopter, and said, "I have forty-five minutes, show me the best parts." I began telling the sales pitch I had rehearsed, but he just waved me away. I shut up then. My boss, who is also my brother, had been clear that the richest man in the world didn't like to have things explained if he didn't ask for them.

My brother hadn't said that the richest man in the world wouldn't ask about *anything*.

"So much storage space!" the girlfriend exclaimed, in reference to the three or four cupboards she had opened. This was an unexpected turn of events. It was her most substantial contribution to the conversation by far. But neither I nor the richest man in the world had anything to say in response. So we didn't.

We closed all the cupboards and walked across the dining room to a set of beautiful French doors that opened onto the lagoon area. The middle of the artificial island was a perfectly circular basin of water, half a mile wide. It could be used for both recreation and aquaculture. Underneath the ring on which the buildings stood, there were nets to isolate the lagoon from the open ocean, but also to automatically catch fish, as well as provide support for growing algae, two important sources of nutrition if the global trade routes were to be disrupted in the event of civilizational collapse. It was extremely ingeniously designed. Of course, I didn't mention any of this out loud. I just said, "Isn't the water a beautiful blue?"

The girlfriend agreed. She said, "mmh-mmh."

The richest man in the world nodded as he stood on the brim of the lagoon. He was looking straight ahead. Greenhouse 2A and Residential Unit 5 were visible on the far side. I wondered what he was thinking about. This was a man who had made a fortune revolutionizing consumer electronics, and then invested almost all of that fortune into revolutionizing social media, and *then* went on to revolutionize the satellite industry, or at least was currently in the process of doing that. There was absolutely no way that his thoughts weren't among the most interesting thoughts in the world. You didn't get this rich having a dull inner life.

He stooped, touched the water. "It's nice," he said. "Do you think we could take a dip?"

"Uh, well, but, I mean, yes, of course, sir," I said. I almost lost my countenance there. He had asked an actual question!

Minutes later, all three of us were wearing Los Jardines, inc.-branded bathsuits. The company had been prepared for the eventuality of "a dip," of course. The girlfriend was floating around on a Los Jardines, inc.-branded inflatable pink flamingo. The richest man in the world and I were lounging on what could be described as the poolside. We were silent. I could have *killed* for an interesting conversation. We were on the first self-sustaining disaster-proof artificial island, for Christ's sake! A marvel of technology and design, built specifically for the ultra-rich to serve as a mobile seasteading base! Why didn't he seem to care whatsoever?

In the end I didn't kill. But I *did* fuck up.

"So, you're gonna buy it, right?"

The richest man in the world looked at me with polite interest.

"You're going to buy this artificial island," I continued, "and then just move here if climate change destroys the world or whatever?"

"I'm still undecided," the richest man in the world said.

"I guess you have other options. You could go to that orbiting space colony they say your company is secretly building."

"Are you not supposed to try to sell me this thing?"

"Sure," I said. "But either way, I lose, right? If you don't buy it, the company—my brother's company—probably goes under, because with the current economic downturn, we don't really have any other interested buyers. If you do buy it, then I get to keep my job, but when climate disaster strikes I'm still fucked."

The richest man in the world seemed genuinely confused.

"Why are you here, then, selling artificial islands to billionaires?"

"Long story," I said. I was about to tell the tale of how I went from poor literature graduate living with roommates in a crappy apartment with awful water pressure at age 32, to getting this job out of pity from my brother, to being the only person who could welcome the potential buyer for complicated reasons involving a ski accident, two infectious diseases, and a high-stakes phone conversation between my brother and I, in which I had to swear that my left-wing anti-bourgeois tendencies were long gone—but the richest man in the world just nodded, as if 'long story' was satisfactory to him. Ugh, why was he so *boring*?

The flamingo-cum-girlfriend floated in our vicinity. "I like the lagoon," the girl said with an accent that appeared to be selected for maximal sexiness. "Are you buying it?"

"Don't know yet, honey," the richest man in the world said.

"Mmh," the girlfriend said.

I couldn't take it anymore. I addressed the girlfriend. "Hey, uh..."

"Yasmina," she said.

"Yasmina. Do you think it's fair? That your... boyfriend might just buy this place and escape the problems that plague the rest of the world?"

"I think it's good to escape problems," Yasmina said.

The mildly gyrating currents of the lagoon pushed the flamingo and Yasmina away before I could think of something to say.

The richest man in the world and I were quiet again. At that point it was probably still possible to minimize the damage. I could have apologized for asking pesky questions. Even just shutting up would probably have done it. But that would have been more boring than I could take, so instead I said, "Do you think she's right? Do you think it's wise to escape problems instead of trying to solve them?"

I saw the faintest hint of irritation on the face of the richest man in the world. "You think I don't work on solving problems?" he said.

"Well, some would say you're creating problems, even. Your rocket launches emit more carbon than the entire airline industry, or something like that."

"That is inaccurate, but even if it weren't, surely you can imagine that we're aware of the environmental cost of all of our activities. In fact, we're expending quite a lot of resources mitigating and offsetting them."

His annoyance was now plainly visible. I clearly knew on some level that I was fucking up. I could already foresee that I would be fired by my brother very soon. And yet—I had gotten the billionaire talking! He was actually saying interesting things! How could I stop, now?

"I mean, you could also just not send all those satellites up there. I get that they're useful, but surely they're not worth making the atmosphere unlivable."

"Please," he said, and made an exasperated face. "If we're actually going to debate this, the least you could do is not be boring. You just rephrased the same argument from a minute ago. We've already been over this."

*He* was accusing me of being a bore? The nerve!

"We have *not* already been over this," I said. "My point is that you're making a fortune doing things that are slowly destroying the planet, and your response seems to be to shop for disaster-proof islands where you can live comfortably even if things go south, when you could just *not* do the planet-destroying things instead."

"Says the guy who works as a salesman for disaster-proof islands."

"That's an ad hominem! Obviously I'm aware of the irony in my work situation. That doesn't mean I can't logically think for myself."

The richest man in the world sighed. "Of course, I'm sorry. You're right," he said. He placed his reasonably muscular arms on the side of the lagoon, and pushed himself out of the water. I followed him. Yasmina was still floating on her flamingo, halfway between here and Residential Unit 2. She seemed to have fallen asleep.

"You're right," the richest man in the world repeated as he dried himself with a Los Jardines, inc.-branded towel, "but then surely you understand that *I* can think for myself, too. I send satellites up in space because I think the satellites improve people's lives on net. The market agrees with me, which is why the company is worth several billion."

I grabbed a towel too. "Of course *you* think markets can never fail."

"No, I'm saying that the markets provide evidence that many people agree with me. They might be wrong. *I* might be wrong. I worry about this constantly. And if the markets had reason to believe that the satellites were now causing more harm, including harm to the climate, than the benefits they bring, then the value of my company would drop. I would notice the signal, and then I would correct my course. I'd work on mitigating and offsetting the problems even more, or even stop the rocket launches altogether if there were no other option."

"Sorry, but that just sounds like a convenient excuse," I said. "You're saying, 'I might be wrong, but I'm not, so I can just do whatever I was going to do anyway.'"

"At some point you have to weigh the pros and cons and commit to something. I could say the same of you. You worry that selling me this artificial island might cause harm, but at the end of the day, you think the benefits of having the job outweigh the risks."

"I have to earn a living somehow—"

"Sure, and I think your job is a useful one."

"—but *you* don't need to earn a living. You're already independently wealthy."

"Yes, I suppose I could just retire, donate most of my fortune to environment-friendly causes, and do nothing for the rest of my life. Is that what you think I should be doing?"

"Well, maybe."

"I don't know. Sounds boring."

I opened my mouth, but said nothing. I couldn't really argue with that. The richest man in the world shrugged and went into the Residential Unit building to change out of his Los Jardines, inc.-branded swimwear. I stayed there for a few minutes, somewhat dumbstruck.

In the lagoon, Yasmina, having realized that we were gone, was trying to steer the flamingo towards us. She made pathetic little paddle motions with her hands. She wasn't getting anywhere. I went back into the water to help her.

The visit was supposed to show Greenhouse 1 next. The greenhouses were the pride and joy of my brother, who despite being a tech founder had always dreamed of being a farmer. There was a reason the company was named after a mythical island covered with lush gardens. So much care had been put into the intricate biological and hydrological systems to grow food in case of global apocalypse. The richest man in the world, my brother thought, would be easily swayed.

But when we were all dressed up and I tried to lead Yasmina and him towards the doors to Greenhouse 1, the richest man in the world spoke.

"I think we're done here," he said. "Thank you very much for the excellent visit. Your team has done great work."

That's when I knew for sure I had fucked up.

The richest man in the world and his girlfriend went back to the helicopter. Once they had boarded, he waved at me. I knew I would never talk with him again.

I remained alone on the giant man-made atoll, a marvellous technological achievement, a feat of engineering and design like no other. The greenhouses glistened under the lowering Hawaiian sun. The lagoon was the most perfect turquoise blue you could imagine.

It was probably the last time I'd see any of it. My brother would certainly fire me, now that I had failed at the most important task. I would be back to square one, with no job and no money, and damaged family ties to boot. And yet the only thing I could think of, as I walked back to the bridge to the mainland, was: the richest man in the world—had I *bored* him?
