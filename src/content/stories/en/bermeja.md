***

1996\. An unassuming fishing boat is cruising the quiet morning waters of the Gulf of Mexico. No other watercraft is nearby. Straight ahead, a very small island—no, a rock, barely rising over the surface of the sea.

The fishing boat's name is printed in blue letters on the white hull: *Dolores*. A small Mexican flag flutters atop her cabin. She looks perfectly ordinary. A clever observer might, however, notice a few irregular details.

First, she's coming from the northwest. Yet the closest shore is that of the Yucatan, to the southeast. Indeed the boat is not Mexican, despite her name and flag. During the night, she left from a small harbor in the south of Texas. Her presence in the territorial waters of Mexico is illegal.

Onboard: about twenty men. All of them American. Half are professional divers. None have ever worked as fishermen on the high seas.

There is not a single piece of fishing equipment on board the *Dolores*.

There is, however, much of something else: explosives.

***

Not too far north of the current position of the *Dolores*, there exists something called a *donut hole*, or *hoyo de dona* in Spanish. It is a region of the sea that belongs to no country, yet is surrounded by territorial waters, in this case those of Mexico and the United States. The boundaries of this particular donut hole are not precisely defined. They depend in principle on the proximity of the land masses belonging to either country, but the governments have not signed any treaty on this specific matter as of yet.

As it happens, an American company has just discovered an important oil field under the waters of the donut hole.

***

The *Dolores* slows down and stops near Bermeja Island. It is a small thing—a rock, barely rising over the surface of the sea—and yet it projects Mexican sovereignty to hundreds of miles around it. If it didn't exist, the United States could claim a large portion of the oil field.

Someone passed this information along to the CIA. The CIA replied with a question: was it possible to make Bermeja Island stop existing?

It was.

The sun is high in the sky when the divers begin putting on their suits. The oxygen tanks await in a line on one side of the boat. The explosive charges are on the other.

Right at the moment the first diver enters the water, a great blue marlin leaps out of the sea. His glistening body follows a graceful curve before he elegantly plunges near the *Dolores*. He is a fortunate fish: no one will attempt to catch him today.
